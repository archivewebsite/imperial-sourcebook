# Practical Grammar Notes

These notes are practical reminders. They avoid tense tables and formal syntax-rule pages.

| Focus | Practical Point | Example | Indonesian Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nouns | German nouns are capitalized. | `das Buch`, `die Stadt` | Kapital membantu mengenali kata benda. |
| Articles | Learn nouns with `der`, `die`, or `das`. | `der Tisch`, `die Frage`, `das Haus` | Artikel membawa gender. |
| Cases | Cases show the role of a noun in the sentence. | `der Mann`, `den Mann` | Article may change when role changes. |
| `kein` vs `nicht` | Use `kein` to negate many nouns with no article or an indefinite article. | `Ich habe keinen Stift.` | `nicht` negates other elements. |
| `nicht` position | `nicht` often stands before what it negates or near the end. | `Ich verstehe nicht.` | Learn from examples, not only rules. |
| Prepositions | Many prepositions carry case patterns. | `mit dem Bus`, `für dich` | Learn phrase and case together. |
| Two-way prepositions | Some prepositions differ by motion or location. | `in die Schule`, `in der Schule` | Direction vs location matters. |
| Verb position | The finite verb is very important for understanding the sentence. | `Heute lerne ich Deutsch.` | Topic can come first; verb stays early. |
| Verb bracket | Modal or separable verbs can place a verb part later. | `Ich muss lernen.`, `Ich stehe auf.` | Wait for the end of the clause. |
| Separable verbs | Prefix may move away in main clauses. | `Ich kaufe ein.` | Store the verb as `einkaufen`. |
| Modal verbs | Modals often pair with an infinitive. | `Ich kann kommen.` | Main action often appears as infinitive. |
| `Sie` vs `du` | `Sie` is formal; `du` is familiar. | `Sprechen Sie Deutsch?` | `Sie` is safest with strangers. |
| Compound nouns | Long nouns can combine smaller nouns. | `der Bahnhof`, `die Hausaufgabe` | The last noun controls gender. |
| Plurals | Plural forms are lexical and should be learned. | `das Buch`, `die Bücher` | Jangan tebak hanya dari gender. |
| `es gibt` | Use `es gibt` for `there is/are`. | `Es gibt ein Problem.` | Literally different from Indonesian. |
