This is a **self-concept system**, basically a set of rules for how you train your brain to see you. Not in a mystical “the universe is your personal delivery app” way, but in a practical psychological way: your repeated self-talk becomes your default identity. Humans, somehow, need rules to stop bullying themselves in their own skulls. Tragic, but useful.

The main idea is: **you are refusing to build your identity around shame, comparison, self-insults, or anxiety.** Instead, you’re deliberately practicing self-respect until it becomes normal.

“**Never speak bad about myself, even jokes**” means you stop making self-deprecating humor your personality. A joke like “I’m so stupid” may feel harmless, but repeated enough, your brain starts treating it like evidence. The rule is not “never be humble.” It means don’t casually disrespect yourself.

“**Accept every compliment, even if I don’t believe it**” means you stop rejecting kindness. When someone compliments you, the practice is to say “thank you” instead of arguing with them. You do not have to fully believe the compliment yet. You just have to stop training yourself to reject positive feedback like it’s spam mail.

“**Compliment myself in the mirror every time I see myself**” is mirror work. It builds familiarity with seeing yourself positively. At first it can feel fake or awkward, because apparently humans can look at themselves daily and still act like saying one nice thing is a federal crime. The key is to use believable compliments if big ones feel too much. For example: “I look nice today,” “I’m proud of myself for trying,” or “I deserve to be treated well.”

“**Never shame myself for having anxiety or stressing**” is very important. Anxiety is not a character flaw. It is your nervous system trying to protect you, sometimes very badly, like an overdramatic security guard. This rule says: instead of thinking “what’s wrong with me?” you think “I’m anxious, but I can handle this.” You separate the feeling from your identity.

“**Be inspired by others, never envious or jealous. If they can do it, so can I**” means you use other people’s success as proof, not punishment. Instead of “they have it and I don’t,” the mindset becomes “that exists, so it’s possible.” This protects you from comparison spirals.

“**Nothing is above me. I’m worthy of anything and everyone**” means you stop putting people, opportunities, lifestyles, or dreams on a pedestal. It does **not** mean entitlement, like “everyone owes me attention.” It means basic worthiness: you are allowed to want good things, apply for big opportunities, date someone you admire, enter rooms you used to feel too small for, and not shrink yourself automatically.

The system works best when it is **firm but not punishing**. If you break a rule and insult yourself, do not insult yourself for insulting yourself. That would be the brain doing a stupid little loop. Just correct it:

“I’m not stupid. I’m learning.”

“I’m anxious, not weak.”

“I don’t need to compare. I can be inspired.”

“I accept the compliment.”

The real purpose is to make self-respect your default setting. Not arrogance. Not delusion. Just refusing to treat yourself like someone you’re stuck tolerating.
