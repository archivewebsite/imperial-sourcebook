# You Cannot Save Someone Who Loves Drowning

## you can’t let the light in for the person who loves the dark

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*Su9-CGz0Oa9BJwUtjZ5GEg.jpeg)

We want to hold onto things we love, and sometimes what we love is the thing that’s killing us slowly. The universe itself moves towards chaos. It is creating complexities in solving them.

You can’t save a person who loves chaos. ==Some people just don’t want to be saved because they are so comfortable in the reality they have created.== You can’t rescue people who are committed to self-destruction habits, toxic situations, or their own misery.

For some, admitting that life is lovely and that this is a great time to be alive is like losing a war. The world that hurt them is at war with them at all times. The idea that everything is perfect hurts them because they are demeaning their own suffering, as the world and its people deeply broke them.

They are not forgetting and forgiving. Instead, they are clinging to the suffering they endured. Because the world caused them pain, they will never see it as a lovely place. ==Giving up on all of the suffering they endured is necessary to acknowledge the beauty of the world==.

Nothing is going to change as long as you have to force it every time. At first, forcing is necessary, but with time it will become a liability, and you will only hurt yourself if you don’t know when to stop. Trying to help them is humanity, and it’s what we are supposed to do.

==Helping once is sympathy, helping twice is love, and helping thrice is enabling, and beyond that is like building a cage for both of you. It’s all about understanding that you can’t take someone to the finish line if they are not willing to take the first step.==

*“I can fix them.”*

*“I will never give up on them.”*

*“* ==*I will keep on trying till my last breath.*==*”*

Try again because giving up too easily isn’t true love, but sometimes giving up can be the final act of love. The best you can do in love is to leave them in whatever shape they are comfortable in. Don’t go beyond that because responsibility begins when compassion ends.

Helping people is what we are made for, but there is a clear boundary that indicates when to stop. You can help, but there are some limits, and you can’t go beyond that.

The idea that you can’t save someone who loves chaos isn’t truly about giving up. This idiom emphasizes that your empathy should not come at the cost of your own mental health or peace. A wounded mind can’t heal another wounded soul.

==If we follow the idiom, it’s like you can’t save a drowning person if you don’t know how to swim. You don’t want to drown trying to save them. There’s no use leading them to the shore if they are used to the depth of the water.==
