## And didn’t touch it after. Don’t get exposed.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*OwODP6Y8PwKsDluhGN1sIQ.png)

Image by Author

Reading just one sentence of your article, I can guess with near certainty that you used ChatGPT and didn’t read or edit it before publishing.

Yes, just one sentence, that you’ve probably written today.

***This may look like:***

*“But here’s the thing.” OR “The truth is, it’s not as simple as it seems.”* Maybe, *“Here’s what’s actually happening”*

This is the real sign of AI. It is not the ideas nor any sentence structure. This one type of sentence, which introduces the next sentence, is a clear indication of ChatGPT use when nobody reads and edits after it.

==***I know this because I’ve been the person who didn’t clean up in the early days of AI.***==

You already know these sentences. You may have written some of them.

***“This is where things get interesting.”***

***“Here’s the truth”***

***“Now, let’s dive into why this actually matters.”***

***“And that brings us to the real question.”***

***“Before we go further, it’s worth understanding…”***

==Read one of these slowly. Try reading== ==*“Before we go further, it’s worth understanding.”*== ==Then remove every word of it. The paragraph after it becomes stronger.== ==When you read again, you realize nothing important is lost, only that guiding sentence that was unnecessary.==

These sentences feel like writing. They are easy to read and easy to accept. That’s why they often look at writing, and that’s also what exposes you.

### Why ChatGPT keeps writing them

Because it is trained on larger data, mostly written by humans, it tries to follow the idea that good writing has a flow, ideas need to connect, and readers shouldn’t feel alone between thoughts. This is the reason every time it feels there is a gap between two ideas, it fills it with the same words or sentences that look bad. That connecting sentence is smooth, seamless and grammatically correct.

***But seamless doesn’t mean alive.***

Human writers try different things and different ideas each time they write. They use a hard cut between two ideas to make both sharper and more memorable, so the reader can make the jump without losing flow. That white space is not a problem to solve. It’s like a tool.

While ChatGPT was never taught to make or trust the gap. So it closes everyone where it notices.

***Look at the same paragraph, before and after.***

This is real ChatGPT output, lightly rephrased:

> Now that we’ve established why consistency matters in content creation, it’s time to explore what that actually looks like in practice. This is where things get interesting. When you build a system around your writing process, something unexpected happens — you stop waiting for inspiration and start manufacturing it.

There are three sentences. The first one is a bridge. The second is also a bridge announcing the bridge. Third is the actual idea, and it’s good. ==But it’s been made to wait behind two sentences of nothing.==

***Now the same paragraph after deleting the bridges:***

> ==Build a system around your writing process and something unexpected happens. You stop waiting for inspiration. You start manufacturing it.==

Same idea explained in fewer words. If you read the idea starts instantly, without any introduction, and that makes it stronger. The cuts didn’t damage anything.

==Those extra layers of sentences before the focus point hide strong ideas.== The ideas don’t need a long setup to be told.

### The writers who never get caught do one thing

They carefully read the draft and always delete every transition sentence before anyone reads it.

==Then they think and rewrite the joins with their own hand, their own rhythm and style, and with the sense of where one idea ends and another starts.== The rewrite that occurs after you delete the bridge is always better than what was there. Because now you’re not just filling space. You’re making a decision and choosing your voice.

### How I found this out

After almost six months of using ChatGPT for everything, I published an article I was proud of because of its real arguments and research I did and spent some extra time writing a hook. I sent it to the senior writer whose opinion I trusted.

He sent back one line: *“It is like a draft that was never really touched.”*

I was shocked by the answer. I sat with that for a while and didn’t reply immediately.

I was staring at it and thinking about it, why it didn’t feel like mine. Then I read the article again and again.

I detected and also sought help from AI, which suggested me to learn about bridge sentences. I researched and found that the bridge sentences like *“This raises an important question,”* “ *Let’s explore what that looks like in practice”,* *“Now that we understand X, we can turn to Y”* were everywhere in that piece. The ChatGPT written structure was visible. Every part feels connected in an obvious way, like someone who is nervously explaining every step instead of letting it flow.

I’d written the hook. body, example and conclusion. But I didn’t touch the transitions. The transitions are the parts where the voice was supposed to be. Everything else was decoration.

I thanked my mentor, and after that, I started checking the bridge or connector sentences, which made my writing better and gave me space to include my own voice and thinking into articles or stories. It takes time, but it is worth it.

In the final review, I ask one question to myself for every transition: Does this sentence add meaning, or does it only introduce meaning???  
If it is introduced, I remove it without any hesitation.

### How to run this on your own work

Reading is the key, but don’t read your draft for quality or for checking the flow. Read it track specifically for sentences that you can delete without taking any information with them.

First mark everyone, then count them. Once you did this, you also know what to fix now, and it’s not the ideas. The ideas are fine. It’s the extra sentences glued between them that weren’t really yours.

ChatGPT will keep writing bridge sentences. That’s not going to change because it’s not human. But now you know what to look for, what it means, and how to deal with it when you find one.

It means that part of the piece is still waiting for you to think and write your own ideas, and your readers already know.
