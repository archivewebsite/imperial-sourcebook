# 8 Uncomfortable Quotes Worth Knowing

Some things I read feel like a psychological colonoscopy. The words creep up on me from behind and illuminate parts within. ==Parts I might not have been able to see by myself.==

The following 8 quotes have had a similar effect in my life. They were uncomfortable to read at first (even confronting), but after some time, I found myself returning to them again and again, and they have now become words I live by.

I’m excited to share them with you here.

## 1\. “If everyone likes you, it probably means you aren’t saying much.” — Donna Brazile

A part of why I was so anxious growing up was that I was worried about what other people thought of me. I avoided conflict like the plague as I wanted to be liked by everyone I met.

My plan worked, but it came at the cost of my mental and physical health.

Writing has been one of the best teachers in breaking this trend because it’s impossible to please everyone all of the time. Someone, somewhere, for no apparent reason, will not like what I say. So I either stop writing altogether or continue and learn how to take criticism and rejection.

I was quiet and shy for way too long. Now I try to speak honestly and authentically and take whatever comes my way as a result.

## 2\. “You can choose courage, or you can choose comfort, but you cannot choose both.” — Brené Brown

This one still gets me because vulnerability is scary. The easiest thing to do would be to remain comfortable and not say anything. But as I mentioned earlier, that came at the cost of my mental and physical health, so it’s not exactly like that’s a good option either.

==If I want to be myself in the world, I have to choose courage over comfort every time. It’s scary, but the alternative is even scarier.==

## 3\. “The trouble is, you think you have time.” — Jack Kornfield

This feels like it’s gently whispered from the Gods as a reminder to live life.

Most of us think we’ve got infinite time to follow our dreams, repair friendships, and say how we really feel to loved ones. ==The saddest part is that we might have a few good years left, but that’s about it.==

Initially, this made me live with a kind of frenetic urgency. I feared missing out on life, so I did everything I possibly could. However, as you can imagine, this was pretty unsustainable.

What I know now is that it’s less about what I do and more about how I do it. Presence is the ultimate goal. I want to bear witness to life, see and feel and hear and touch and smell as much as I can while my five senses are still intact. I want to experience each and every breath move in and out of my lungs before I can’t breathe anymore. If anything, Jack Kornfield’s words make me slow down, not speed up.

## 4\. “What are you afraid of losing, when nothing in the world belongs to you.” — Marcus Aurelius

It’s not like I came into this world with an Apple laptop, a British passport, and a 2018 4x4 Renault Duster. It’s not like I will leave this world with any of those things either.

Everything in life is temporary.

Somehow this makes me appreciate the things I have more exactly because they (and I) have an expiry date.

## 5\. “The desire to be loved is the last illusion. Give it up and you will be free.” — Margaret Atwood

One of the greatest lessons I learned in life is that peace and love are cultivated within. It doesn’t matter if someone loves me unconditionally because if I don’t love myself, I can’t feel it.

When I realised this, I stopped seeking validation outside of myself and started asking why I was seeking validation in the first place. This began a four-year-long exploration into my heart and psyche that ended up with me finally accepting and loving who I was.

Margaret Atwood makes this process sound incredibly easy and straightforward. In my experience, it’s the opposite. However, she is right about giving up the desire to be loved because it redirects the energy and turns it within. That’s when things really begin to change.

## 6\. “All sins are attempts to fill voids.” — Simone Weil

Over the years, I’ve numbed myself out with tobacco, alcohol, drugs, and work. At my lowest point, I used all four at the same time to gloss over the emptiness I felt inside.

It felt impossible to give them up, too, because they were something to look forward to, and they provided relief from the world.

“What do you think is in there, Andy A big scary monster” My tantra teacher teased. “You’re not as scary as you think you are.”

I had been avoiding myself for so many years, and I’d used all kinds of different vices to help me do so. But with my teacher’s words reverberating in my ears, I signed up for a 10-day Vipassana meditation course, left everything I owned at the door, and sat down with myself for the first time in my life.

It turns out he was right: I’m not that scary, after all.

> *“When you can bear your own silence, you are free.” — Mooji*

## 7\. “What goes too long unchanged destroys itself. The forest is forever because it dies and dies and so lives.” — Ursula K. Le Guin

Whatever is true for a forest is true for the human spirit. We must change and grow; otherwise, the psyche has a funny way of creating problems to do it, anyway.

Many times, that means letting old versions of ourselves die in order to become something new.

## 8\. “The only person coming to save you is the version of yourself that’s tired of your current situation.” — Tiny Buddha

I know hate is a strong word, but I used to hate this sentence. It felt unfair. When I was at rock bottom, I wanted someone to scoop me up and tell me everything would be ok. I wanted someone to save me.

I certainly didn’t want to be told that I was the only person who could dig myself out of the hole I was in.

But I was fed up and exhausted. So after a few days moping around, ==I decided it was time to change. There was no other way.==

I read all the books I could get my hands on to learn how to save, invest, and think about money to get myself out of debt.

I stopped eating gluten, dairy, sugar, and processed foods to try to heal my gut.

I started meditating and practicing breathwork to soothe my anxious mind. And I started writing online to share my experiences with the world.

Jim Rohn said, “We generally change ourselves for ==on== e of two reasons: inspiration or desperation.”

==Whatever the reason, it comes from within, and it’s a powerful force.==
