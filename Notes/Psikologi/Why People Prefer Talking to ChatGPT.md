## My friend was angry with me because I wasn’t angry enough

Recently, I had a mildly annoying work situation. ==I’m the type of person who likes talking things out with others to relieve stress.==

I should have known better, though. I made the mistake of talking to a friend who is not exactly the best listener in the world. Actually, that is an understatement.

As soon as I started sharing the story with her, I knew it was a mistake. Her lips started twitching as if she was ready to say something the moment I paused between sentences. As expected, before I could even finish the full story, she saw an opening and jumped right in.

She immediately told me what I should have done or said. The thing is, I wasn’t looking for a solution. I just wanted to talk it out. In other words, I just wanted her to listen to me rant.

So I stopped her and told her that I just wanted to vent and that I wasn’t really all that angry. Maybe she could just listen for a bit, and then I’d be fine.

Bad idea.

She became very angry and asked if I wanted to continue being treated that way at work. The thing is, it was actually just a small matter that I was annoyed about. ==But she was so mad on my behalf.==

She then got mad at me when she realised I wasn’t going to do anything beyond ranting. She was so furious that I wasn’t as angry as she thought I should be!

She then started talking about her own issues, which didn’t even resemble mine or maybe just slightly at best. She was really projecting to the max. She kept giving examples of her own bad situations, and I kept telling her they were not related at all. It was exhausting.

After a while, I was so confused about how any of it was related to my own issue. When I pointed that out, she nearly exploded and asked if I actually liked being mistreated. That was completely rude, irrelevant and unrelated.

In the end, she made me so stressed that the work situation seemed much milder in comparison. I can’t believe she was angry with me for not being angry enough.

It’s really my own fault, though. I should have known better than to talk to her just because she was there in person with me. ==She just didn’t want to listen.== Or maybe she didn’t have the capacity to. I’m not sure.

That got me thinking that sometimes it’s just better to talk to ChatGPT.

Now, before anyone comes after me, no, I’m not saying that we should replace human connections with AI.

I completely understand that ChatGPT has limitations, and for people who are really suffering from mental health issues, they should never rely on ChatGPT. A trained professional is always the best option. In fact, it should be the only option for those dealing with serious mental health issues.

==In my situation, I just wanted to vent, and for what it’s worth, that bad listener friend is also not a trained professional. At least when I vent to ChatGPT, it doesn’t suddenly get angry on my behalf and then get angry with me for not being angry enough.==

That, to me, was the weirdest part.

==Would you agree that talking to AI is better in this case?==
