## Our daily political update, featuring the stories that matter

![U.S. Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) speaks during a news conference following a weekly policy luncheon with Senate Republicans at the U.S. Capitol.](https://www.economist.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=1424,quality=80,format=auto/content-assets/images/20260523_dap302.jpg)

In a rare act of defiance to **Donald Trump**, Senate Republicans postponed a vote on a bill funding his immigration crackdown because some objected to his [“Anti-Weaponisation Fund”](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2026/05/19/even-by-trumpian-standards-a-18bn-fund-for-friends-is-bad). Senators said officials had not explained who could receive payouts and whether people convicted of crimes related to the storming of the Capitol on January 6th 2021 might benefit. Party leaders had already removed funding tied to Mr Trump’s White House ballroom from the immigration bill, for fear the rank-and-file would not support it.

---

Mr Trump abruptly postponed the signing of an executive order that would have expanded federal oversight of **artificial-intelligence** models, including by requiring AI firms to share new models with the government before release. David Sacks, Mr Trump’s former AI adviser, reportedly helped persuade the president to pull back. Mr Trump said it “gets in the way” of [America’s tech race with China](https://www.economist.com/china/2026/05/07/ai-creates-a-fearsome-cold-war-style-dilemma).

---

As Mr Trump was arguing that AI was “bringing in a lot of jobs”, **Gavin Newsom**, California’s Democratic governor, ordered state agencies to prepare for “potential layoffs and economic disruption” [caused by AI](https://www.economist.com/leaders/2026/05/14/prepare-for-an-ai-jobs-apocalypse). California’s state agencies will explore new unemployment-insurance and severance policies for displaced workers.

---

Republican leaders in the **House of Representatives** cancelled a vote on whether to limit Mr Trump’s ability to wage [war on Iran](https://www.economist.com/middle-east-and-africa/2026/05/21/donald-trump-is-still-looking-for-a-quick-fix-in-iran) without congressional approval after concluding the measure might pass. Several Republicans had defected on a similar vote last week, which failed in a 212-212 tie. This week several Republican senators backed a similar measure.

---

**Stephen Colbert** hosted his final episode of CBS ’s “The Late Show” after 11 years at the helm of one of America’s best-known talk shows. In the Trump era, Mr Colbert’s acerbic monologues helped him top the ratings. His cancellation was controversial; critics accused CBS of yielding to pressure from the president.

## A view from elsewhere

In *Corriere della Sera*, an **Italian** newspaper, [Federico Fubini](https://www.corriere.it/opinioni/26_maggio_11/trump-e-rimasto-solo-46b27ee9-4930-4961-bc4d-5ac39b024xlk.shtml) argues that America’s artificial-intelligence boom depends more on foreign goodwill than many in Washington realise. As firms such as Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Meta pour vast sums into AI, they will need open overseas markets to justify those investments and fend off cheaper Chinese rivals. Yet Mr Trump’s foreign policy, he argues, is alienating allies at precisely the wrong moment, pushing even close partners to deepen ties with China.

## Daily quiz

![](https://www.economist.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=1424,quality=80,format=auto/content-assets/images/20240129_OPD001.jpg)

Illustration: Sandra Navarro

From Monday to Thursday we’ll quiz you on all things American. The winner of this week’s quiz, on AI, is Christopher Lewis. The answers are:

**Monday:** At which American university was the term “artificial intelligence” coined in 1956

At Dartmouth College in New Hampshire, where a summer workshop helped establish AI as an academic field.

**Tuesday:** Which American company built the computer that defeated the world chess champion, Garry Kasparov, in 1997

IBM. Its Deep Blue supercomputer beat Mr Kasparov in a six-game match.

**Wednesday:** Which 1960s computer program, developed at an American university, mimicked a psychotherapist and inspired today’s chatbots

ELIZA, created by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT. It gave the illusion of conversation by rephrasing users’ statements as questions.

**Thursday:** Which American state was first to pass a law regulating deepfakes in political campaigns

Texas, which in 2019 banned deceptive deepfake videos intended to influence elections.
