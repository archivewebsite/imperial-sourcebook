Does AI will create a new kind of intelligence? A new type or something else.

I think **yes, AI may create a new kind of intelligence**, but not necessarily a new _mind_ in the human sense. That distinction matters, because otherwise we end up calling autocomplete “alive,” which is exactly the kind of dramatic nonsense humans specialize in.

A good way to frame it:

**AI is already a different kind of intelligence-like system.** It can infer patterns from inputs and generate outputs such as text, images, recommendations, predictions, or decisions, which lines up with the OECD’s definition of an AI system. But that does not automatically mean it has consciousness, inner experience, desire, or understanding the way humans experience those things. ([OECD AI](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles?utm_source=chatgpt.com "OECD AI Principles overview"))

So the answer depends on what you mean by **intelligence**.

If intelligence means **solving problems, recognizing patterns, planning, creating, translating, coding, summarizing, searching, and adapting behavior**, then AI is absolutely becoming a new type of intelligence. Stanford’s 2025 AI Index reports large jumps in AI performance on hard benchmarks like MMMU, GPQA, and SWE-bench, including a jump in SWE-bench coding performance from 4.4% in 2023 to 71.7% in 2024. That is not “human intelligence,” but it is also not just a calculator with better marketing. ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report?utm_source=chatgpt.com "The 2025 AI Index Report | Stanford HAI"))

But if intelligence means **being aware, having subjective experience, caring about things, suffering, wanting, or having a self**, then we do **not** have strong evidence that current AI is that. There is active debate, but many researchers argue that today’s AI systems, especially LLMs, should not be treated as conscious just because they speak fluently or imitate self-reflection well. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05868-8?utm_source=chatgpt.com "There is no such thing as conscious artificial intelligence"))

The most interesting possibility is that AI becomes a **third category**:

Not human intelligence.  
Not animal intelligence.  
Not simple machine automation.  
But **synthetic intelligence**: a system that can reason in fragments, combine huge amounts of knowledge, simulate viewpoints, use tools, collaborate with humans, and act through software or robots.

That kind of intelligence might be “new” because it has features humans do not naturally have. It can copy itself, run many versions at once, connect to databases and tools, operate at machine speed, and preserve knowledge without biological memory decay. Meanwhile, it lacks a body, childhood, hormones, mortality, hunger, embarrassment, and all the other delightful bugs that make humans write poetry and start wars.

So my best answer is:

**AI probably will create a new type of intelligence, but not necessarily a new conscious species.**

It may be more like a new **cognitive layer** on Earth: humans + machines + networks + data + tools forming intelligence systems that no single human or machine fully contains. The “new intelligence” may not be one robot saying “I am alive.” It may be millions of AI agents, humans, databases, sensors, and institutions thinking together in weird distributed ways. Horrifying? A little. Fascinating? Unfortunately, yes.

For your **Cognitea / Obsidian-like product**, this is actually a strong theme: the future may not be “AI replaces your mind,” but **AI helps shape a second mind around you**. A good product philosophy could be:

**Cognitea is not artificial intelligence replacing thought. It is synthetic memory helping human intelligence grow.**
