I think I am still not finished with the idea of being afraid to receive kindness. My mind keeps searching for words that can make it feel reasonable. I fear that the wounds on my body, the ones seen and unseen, have blinded me from kindness in its purest form, from the quiet way one person can honor another simply by being gentle, from the truth that sometimes people can be kind without asking to be repaid.

==By the way I have learned to think, I am the bad one. I would rather look for the knife hidden behind someone’s back than accept the peeled apple they hold out in front of them when kindness comes near. What if the apple is only bait, and I am a starving fish in dark water, with only three breaths left to risk.==

I call myself the bad one because cruelty makes more sense to me. It is easy to believe that life can break people until they stop caring how deeply they cut others. If someone hurts me, I understand the language at once. I know where to place the pain. I know how to leave.

But kind people confuse me.

The ones who open their arms without asking for anything first. The ones who offer their shoulder and let their hours disappear just to hear the weight in my voice. The ones who notice what I need before I can name it, then quietly place it near me as if love should arrive without noise.

If I am the bad one, then kindness feels misplaced when it reaches me. I search their hands for hidden motives and stain their softness with my suspicion. I am the one who walks away first while they are still trying to make room for themselves beside me. I am the one who grows tired first while they are still pouring warmth into my cold hands.

I think this is how God punishes my wrongness, by surrounding me with so many good people. people I do not know how to trust.

==Why does kindness feel more threatening than harm.==

Harm is familiar. Harm asks nothing new of me. It only confirms what fear has always whispered. Kindness asks me to loosen my grip, to rest, to believe not every hand was made to strike. That feels harder than survival.

I search through old memories for an answer. Sometimes I think it is because the kind people before them kept a ledger in secret. They gave with one hand and collected with the other. They called debt devotion. They called my gratitude love.

After that, I no longer knew how to recognize kindness, or how to receive it without trembling.

All I have now is fear. Somehow, it protects me.

It lets me move through the world expecting knives, so I never have to mourn another surprise. And maybe good people should only find other good people, not me.

I have lived too long among wounds, and I fear I have learned to bleed on everything gentle. And if I am truly the bad one, then I can live with never being led toward good people at all. I can accept a life of doors that do not open for me. I can accept being left alone again and again.
