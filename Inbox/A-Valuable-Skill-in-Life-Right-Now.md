## The antidote for these difficult times.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*LaZrLAM1HVbEYVjL7CdfCQ.jpeg)

Photo by Saro Contemporary Artist on Unsplash

==All life is suffering. You don’t just know that. You live it. You experience all of it. The pain, dissatisfaction, and the changes you didn’t ask for. “If there is meaning in life at all, then there must be a meaning in suffering.” I agree with Viktor Frankl. But the “root of suffering is attachment,” Buddha said.== That means what can’t be changed need not be endured. If you can step outside the pain. Detach from all the things falling apart. And the worst things happening right now. You have “stepped outside and above,” what you can’t change. That’s something. A valuable skill they don’t teach in school. But they should. The ability to transcend hard times. And keep your sanity, “untroubled” changes your relationship with the many stressors of life. Right now, the pressure to live is no joke. Entire industries are resetting because of tech intelligence.

Economies are struggling because of the escalation in the middle east. The ripple effects are everywhere. Political decisions made far from your life are changing your life anyway. ==And when it gets too much, something happens in your head. The problems expand. It fills the mental space.== And it starts to feel permanent, total, and defining. When these external forces press in, your world becomes all. You feel rattled and reactive. Yes, it’s bad things changing your life in ways you didn’t expect. But that reality we interpret in our heads can do more harm than good. People mistake urgency for importance. You can and will never predict what Trump will do next. The chaos in the world is outside your control. That part doesn’t change. What changes is where you stand in relation to it.

I will tell you what detaching from all of it is not. You’re pretending your problems don’t exist. Your rent going up all the time is real. The job anxiety you feel is all true. The dread you feel reading the news is also real. Becoming bigger than the suffering means you identify with all things bad. You stay separate from it. And narrow your focus to your circle of influence. You work on the immediate problem you can solve. You live from a place of calm and rarely panic. Of course, it’s easier said than done.

The trouble with attachment to chaos is that it shrinks your perspective. When you’re rattled, almost all the time, your brain zooms in. Hard. Whatever is wrong becomes the only thing in view. A difficult conversation feels like the relationship ending. A bad month at work feels like a career collapsing. A bad news cycle feels you are losing control of your life. You become mentally glued to a thought until the thought is all you are. There’s a term for it: cognitive fusion. The antidote is not positive thinking. It’s perspective. Literally stepping back, sometimes physically, to think clearly. Nine times out of ten, you will get back to the knowledge that your self-destructing is happening in your head. You can take back some of the control. The economy is all bad.

But you still have to wake up tomorrow and check things off your to-do list. Jobs are at risk. But still decide how you respond, what you protect, what you refuse to surrender. The Stoics had a word for this. *Prohairesis*. The faculty of choice. Epictetus built his entire life philosophy around it. His judgments, values, or what he decided to do next were all in his control. When a specific life experience is consuming you, name it to contain it. Literally say or write, “I’m being consumed by this right now.” Naming the state creates a tiny distance from it.

That distance is enough to start thinking clearly.

Downsize your time horizon. If the next year feels overwhelming, forget the next year. Audit the “now.” What is happening in your physical space? I am sitting in a chair, reading, or looking at a screen. Everything else is a mental projection of a future that hasn’t happened yet. What does the next hour require from? Do that. Then the hour after. You could also describe your experience as if you were observing someone. Stop saying, “I am failing.” Try “I’m going through a complex career transition right now. I’m doing everything I can.”

It creates immediate breathing room.

Viktor Frankl, surviving Auschwitz, described reducing his mental scope to the smallest survivable unit of time. “Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response,” he said. The economy and the politicians own the “stimulus.” You own the “space.” When you occupy that space, you stop being rattled by the news cycle. You become the one holding the cue. If it worked for Frankl, it works in your home or office. What is within your control today? Write a list. Work only that list. Everything else, the fed’s decisions, the economy going south, the oil wars at the strait of hormuz goes in a separate circle. Things outside your control. Stop spending cognitive time on it. Moving your body helps. A short walk, a few minutes of deliberate breathing. They all take your mind off the things you can’t change. Movement narrows cognition. You literally cannot think broadly when your body is in threat mode.

==Calm the body first, then think.==

Action requires a calm body. You cannot solve a career crisis or transcend a recession while your brain is in “cortisol” mode. By stepping above the experience, you reclaim your cognitive skill to get back to “doing.” Stay curious about things falling apart around you, rather than being intimidated by them. No matter what’s happening outside of your influence, you are still in charge of your next action that affects your life. Seneca was right when he said, “We suffer more often in imagination than in reality.” Your anxiety about the future haunts your future before it even arrives. Don’t do it. “You are the sky. Everything else — it’s just the weather,” says buddhist nun Pema Chödrön.

Remain untouched.

It takes work. It’s a practice. It gets better with time. Your interpretation of everything as “good” or “bad” is all happening in your head. If you change the label, you change your internal peace spectrum. Learning to be larger than all the things you practically can’t change isn’t optional anymore. It’s the skill right now as world is going through reset process. Einstein said, “In the middle of difficulty lies opportunity.” But only for those who are not consumed by the difficulty itself. In tough times remember what Marcus Aurelius said, “The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.” Winning at your life is up to you.
