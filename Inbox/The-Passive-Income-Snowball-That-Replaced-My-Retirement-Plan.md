### The Problem Most Of Us Are Facing

For a long time I followed the crowd when it came to the typical advice of saving for retirement.

> The typical advice: We are told that we need to put away 8–12% of our income into a retirement fund (401k or IRA) and let it build up until this magical day when we retire and at that point, hopefully we’ll have enough of a nest egg to live off for our remaining years.

The experts and pundits say you must have enough of a nest egg that you can withdraw 4% per year and it will last you 30 years. **This is commonly called the 4% rule**.

What doesn’t get talked about is this number needs to be pretty damn big for you to comfortably withdraw 4% and not be living off scraps.

What this actually means is if you want to have $100k per year in income (which isn’t a lot these days), you need to have around $2.5 million saved up.

That doesn’t account for inflation, which in my opinion will continue to rise at greater than 3% per year — so that $2.5 million now becomes $4 million in 2 decades from now.

Lifespans are also increasing, and this will accelerate due to new biotech and AI; I expect people to be regularly living past 100 in the coming decades.

That pushes the number up to $5–6 million.

I personally do not want to be living off a $100k per year salary because I want to be travelling and enjoying life to the fullest.

For me this number looks like I would need around $20 million if I were to be using the 4% rule.

If we are being real, the retirement numbers for those in the 55–65 year range are dismal and a small percentage are pacing for even a $1 million nest egg, let alone $2.5 million.

==The numbers show that this is an unrealistic strategy for most people; it simply doesn’t work unless you’ve started at an early age and saved consistently.==

Starting later, even with a high income like mine still make it a roll of the dice.

These days most people are struggling to even get the bills paid let alone put money away for retirement.

Another problem with this model is the day you stop working, your wealth starts to evaporate like a bucket filled with water and a big hole in it due to your expenses.

I realized this about 10 years ago. I was 41 years old and making a multiple six figure income and the calculations were still dicey to say the least.

I knew I needed another plan and that’s where I started shifting my mind away from **net worth** (nest egg) to **cash flow** (passive income).

Passive income is about creating assets that work for you and throw off cash flow instead of needing to be sold to create income.

Cash flowing assets include things like dividend paying stocks, royalties, real estate.

Later on I realized these assets could also come in the form of digital products or monetizing an audience in the form of sponsorships and affiliate income.

### Creating Financial Escape Velocity

The term **financial freedom** for most people means that you have reached that milestone, the nest egg of $2.5 million for example, where you have enough money saved up to last your remaining years.

Financial freedom can come in other forms as well.

For a lot of wealthy people the strategy involves acquiring assets through life but never liquidating them, they borrow against them (tax free) and then pass them onto their heirs and reset the cost basis so their heirs don’t pay taxes on the gains.

*For me the goal is to create enough passive income that it exceeds the income needed to live.*

For example if you need $100k per year to live on, then instead of saving up $2.5 million, you just create $100k per year in passive income.

When your passive income exceeds your expenses, you’ve created escape velocity and are financially free.

It’s a completely different game when you look at it like this. Instead of focusing on net worth with all kinds of moving variables (inflation, expenses and life expectancy), you simply focus on growing your cash flow.

### From Dividend Paying Stocks To Digital Products

When I first started focusing on passive income, the only thing I could think to do was to buy dividend paying stocks.

I started by putting an extra $100 away every month buying stocks here and there which typically paid a 4–5% dividend, sometimes higher.

As time went on and my income grew, I started buying more and more dividend paying stocks.

It felt incredibly slow in those first years but started picking up more and more momentum and within a few years I was making $4000 per year in passive income.

The problem was it didn’t shift the trajectory all that much because a 4–5% dividend was virtually the same as saving up a nest egg of >$2.5 million and using the 4% rule.

At that point it was a sobering reality that I needed to find new ways of creating more income to fuel that fire.

I started doing some coaching & consulting on the side and put that money away as well.

The real shift came when I started creating online courses which allowed me to sell ‘one to many’ instead of trading my time for money.

Online courses were an early form of a digital product for me; and later I moved into my current strategy of creating things like templates and e-books to sell on Etsy, other marketplaces or my personal website.

Because of digital products, I was able to scale in less than 2 years to be making around $4–5k per month in passive income from the digital products alone.

> ==Think about that for a sec, it took me 5 years of buying dividend paying stocks to make $4k in passive income== ==**per year**== ==— or — 2 years to create digital products that throw off $4–5k per month.==

Combining it all together (real estate, dividend paying stocks, affiliate income and digital products) I’m now making $9–10k per month, or over $100k per year in passive income.

This is what I’m talking about, I don’t have a $2.5 million nest egg but I do have $100k per year in passive income!

This is the shift that I’m talking about: cash flow vs. net worth; and cash flow easily wins.

This doesn’t mean I let off the gas, this means I continue to build out assets with a goal of exceeding $340k per year in passive income before December 31, 2026.

By shifting from net worth to cashflow, I’ve moved my financial escape velocity up by decades and the assets don’t deplete when and if I decide to stop working like they would with a traditional retirement strategy.

### The Passive Income Snowball Strategy Explained

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*XNWgfUn5BN0LpfZYa0d-gw.png)

Passive Income Snowball structure

Financial Guru Dave Ramsey often talks about a concept called the **Debt Snowball** for eliminating personal debt**.**

> The Dave Ramsey Debt Snowball is a debt reduction strategy where you list debts from smallest to largest balance, regardless of interest rate, and pay them off in that order. By paying minimums on all debts except the smallest — which receives all extra funds — you build momentum and motivation through quick wins.

It’s a great strategy for getting out of debt, but here’s the cool thing — it can be used to build your financial independence through passive income.

I call this the **Passive Income Snowball** — in a nutshell it just means never spending any of your passive income (regardless of the source) and using that passive income to buy cash flowing assets (stocks or real estate) instead.

This means your passive income starts to create MORE passive income and it starts compounding over time and making more and more money, gaining momentum like a snowball rolling down the hill and getting bigger and bigger.

It’s essentially the opposite of the Debt Snowball (building compounding passive income instead of paying off debt). Just to be clear, I’m not saying don’t pay off your debt, pay that off first if you have debt.

For someone that doesn’t have any money to put towards buying assets, they just need to start by creating ONE stream of passive income and not spending it, use it to buy an asset instead (such as a dividend paying stock).

Once you have that first passive income stream, move onto the next and then the next and then the next.

When I say passive income stream, it doesn’t even mean having multiple sources, it could also mean building out more and more digital products.

The beauty about digital products is they can be scaled in a lot of different ways — for example creating bundles of already existing digital products or selling in multiple places, or using upsells and order bumps. All the strategies I talk about in my content.

> The key that I cannot stress more is you must not spend your passive income on frivolous things or use it as discretionary income.

**You must have discipline.** Use your regular income to fund your living expenses and discretionary spending. **Use your passive income to buy assets.**

For me, I kind of went the opposite way, I started with dividend paying stocks then bought my first rental property, then my second; and then later added digital products and affiliate income.

While it does work both ways, if you are strapped for income, then start with digital products and/or affiliate income and use that income to start buying assets.

- **Real Estate** — I currently have 2 rental properties, Indiana and Atlanta, both are completely managed by a property management company.

I want to stress that digital products or passive income are not just limited to what I’ve mentioned here, these are just what I focus on.

### Where To Start

I realize that what I’ve talked about here can seem complex and overwhelming but I want you to think just about the basics.

This didn’t start all at once, this is a high level look at what took me a decade to create.

The one thing I would have changed is I would have started with digital products or affiliate income, I just didn’t understand them 10 years ago like I do now.

They are something that anyone can start and you don’t need a lot of money to do it.

I see a lot of people who create a simple PDF to sell on the back of a Medium article.

Keep it simple and just build one thing, don’t worry about it being perfect, just get it up and go through the process.

Be willing to try and to fail, but keep going until you’ve got one successful digital product; even if it just makes a $10–100 per month.

The key is going through that process for the first time, then getting better as you go.

When you make that first $10–100, don’t spend it, put it away. You don’t even need to buy a stock with it. You can put it in a high yield savings account or money market fund.

Then you’ve done the first step, you’ve created some passive income and then put it away to become an asset that can create interest for you (which is also a form of passive income).
