## Imagine waking up tomorrow to discover that machines can drive your car, diagnose your illness, manage your investments, and even write legal contracts — all more effectively than humans. This isn’t science fiction; it’s happening right now. Business leaders today face a stark choice: get swallowed up by the technological wave, or ride its crest to new profits and better business.

Malcolm Frank, Paul Roehrig, and Ben Pring from Cognizant’s Center for the Future of Work wrote this book to answer one urgent question: How do we not just survive, but thrive, when intelligent machines reshape every aspect of work and life?

W *hat To Do When Machines Do Everything* (2017) takes a realistic look at what lies ahead for traditional jobs when industries adopt the next wave of automation: How can automation be incorporated into current business models? What should workers and managers expect? And what will happen to the economy as a whole?

### Understanding the New Machine Age

The world stands at the edge of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, where artificial intelligence, algorithms, and big data are creating a transformation similar to what the steam engine and assembly line brought to previous generations. Malcolm Frank, Paul Roehrig, and Ben Pring from Cognizant’s Center for the Future of Work argue that we’re not heading toward doom, but rather toward the biggest wave of opportunity creation since the Industrial Revolution. The “new machine” isn’t about robots that look human — it’s about **systems of intelligence that combine learning software, massive processing power, and enormous amounts of data** to revolutionize how businesses compete and how people work.

### The AHEAD Framework for Success

The authors present a clear strategy called AHEAD to help organizations and individuals navigate this transformation. **Automate** means outsourcing repetitive computational work to machines, just as Netflix automated away Blockbuster’s business model. **Halos** refers to instrumenting products and people to generate valuable data — turning everything into a data generator that creates new customer experiences. **Enhance** treats computers as colleagues that boost human productivity and job satisfaction, like GPS systems that help truck drivers perform better. **Abundance** uses machine power to drop prices and open vast new markets, making services available to people who couldn’t afford them before. **Discovery** focuses on driving innovation to find new futures and business models.

### Jobs Will Change, Not Disappear

While approximately 12 percent of the American workforce (19 million people) will see their jobs automated, the authors predict that 21 million new jobs will be created by AI. More than 80 percent of teaching, nursing, legal, and coding jobs will become more productive and satisfying as the most boring tasks transfer to machines.

> The key difference is between tasks and jobs — AI will automate individual tasks, but complete jobs require human qualities like creativity, emotional intelligence, and complex problem-solving.

Financial advisors, teachers, lawyers, and healthcare workers will still be essential, but AI will handle the time-consuming research and information gathering, freeing humans to focus on relationships and judgment calls.

### Data Becomes the New Oil

> In this digital economy, data serves as the most valuable raw material, replacing traditional resources like oil and steel.

Businesses must adopt an “instrument everything” attitude, turning every product, process, and interaction into a data generator. Companies that mine and refine data effectively will gain unprecedented insights into customer behavior, improve decision-making, and drive innovation. However, the authors emphasize that data is initially a liability before becoming an asset — organizations need the right tools, strategies, and business models to transform raw data into competitive advantages.

### The Human Touch Matters More Than Ever

Despite machines doing more work, the human element becomes increasingly important in the automated world. Companies like Pret A Manger offer highly efficient service but build their brand on quirky, fun, and engaged staff members who provide the human touch. Apple eliminated traditional checkout desks and empowered floor staff with mobile tools to create personal customer connections. The more technology enhances us, the more opportunities emerge for human qualities that machines cannot replicate — curiosity, creativity, empathy, and the ability to ask “why.” Organizations must double down on activities where humans maintain advantages over silicon, making work more meaningful rather than obsolete.

## 📝 Action Plan

- **Embrace the AHEAD model:** Automate repetitive tasks, instrument everything to generate data, enhance human workers with AI tools, create abundance through lower costs, and foster a culture of discovery.
- Transform your business model to be **data-driven** rather than simply adding technology to existing processes — ask “How would I design my business if this technology existed from day one?”
- Invest in **continuous learning** and **skills development** to adapt to the changing job market, focusing on uniquely human capabilities like creativity and emotional intelligence.
- Set ambitious transformation goals of 25–30% **cost reduction** and similar **revenue increases**, as many organizations already achieve 8–10% impact with initial AI efforts.
- Build systems that enhance human performance rather than replace it, viewing machines as colleagues and coaches that make work more productive and satisfying.

## 🧩 Blind Spots

- The book takes an overwhelmingly optimistic view while glossing over real challenges, particularly the painful transition period when millions lose jobs before new opportunities fully materialize. Workers displaced by automation may struggle financially during this gap, and the book offers limited practical guidance for individuals facing job loss beyond “don’t resist technology.”
- Change management and organizational resistance receive only cursory attention, despite being the biggest roadblock to AI transformation in practice. The authors focus heavily on what CEOs and executives should do but provide minimal actionable advice for middle managers, individual contributors, or workers in industries facing disruption.

## 📌 My Highlights

- So, **automation** isn’t going to take away a teacher’s job; rather, it’ll make the **teacher** more effective.
- Despite the billions invested in the consumer technology of smartphones and apps, as well as business-minded hardware and software of PCs and databases, **productivity** hasn’t changed much. Plus, when you [compare the increase in annual wages in the United States from 1991 to 2012, it was roughly half the increase that took place between 1970 and 1990](https://www.thoughtco.com/us-economy-in-the-1990s-and-beyond-1148149#:~:text=The%201990s%20economy%20grew%20fast,a%20large%20debt%20still%20existed.).
- The paper-based business model is outdated, particularly in the eyes of the **millennial generation**, who expect to open an app and get an answer right away, rather than wait for **paperwork** to make its way through the cubicle convolution.
- To guarantee that your business analysts have **good data** to work with, you should gather that data from every possible product, service and source in your organization — a process called **instrumenting**.
- For example depending on how old you are, you may or may not remember a time when phones couldn’t store numbers or keep track of calls. If today’s phones are considered smart, then these old models could be called dumbphones. The authors believe that, by 2025, we’ll look back at today’s desks, shoes, toothbrushes and doors as being a lot like the dumbphone. We’ll be amazed that there was a time when these things didn’t help us perform our tasks more **efficiently**.
- In other words, we’re on the brink of a smart-product transformation, where miniature data collectors will be installed in just about everything we use. So now’s the time to get ahead of the curve by instrumenting everything in your organization, **harvesting** all possible **data** and finding the intrinsic value in every aspect of your business.
- For example, during the first industrial revolution in the nineteenth century, workers in England, calling themselves the [**Luddites**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luddite#:~:text=The%20Luddites%20were%20members%20of,the%20machines%20in%20organised%20raids.), destroyed the mechanical looms being introduced to the textile industry. The Luddites believed these machines were threatening their jobs — and sure enough, the machines did replace them.
- Also during the early nineteenth century, 80 percent of US workers had a job in agriculture. But that figure has dropped to less than 2 percent due to the machines that perform the major tasks associated with **farming**, tending to livestock and toiling the land.
- However, if you’re going to defend your business from these marauding start-ups, you’ll need to get your hands on one other tool they already have at their disposal: a **digital business model**.
- First of all, it’s important to understand that a **business model** refers to two things: how your **organization** is structured and the processes your organization oversees in order to compete and generate **revenue**.
- There’s a new industrial revolution coming, and the instrument at its core is a system of intelligence powered by self-learning software and massive amounts of data. This new technology will make it possible for many tasks to be automated — but rather than eliminating jobs, this can both create jobs and free up time for employees to improve the quality of their work. The businesses that will succeed in the future will be the ones that **integrate automation and self-learning softwar** e into their business models, and make the most of their **data** to gain a competitive advantage.
- We’re already used to smartphones and watches that track how far we walk each day, how much sleep we get each night and even remind us when it’s time to exercise. Machines can already help us manage our lives, but the digital products of the future will go a step further by taking all of our data and doing our jobs in a better, more efficient and more accurate way than we ever could.
- So, when moving to a digital business model, the question arises: what can you automate? Start by looking around your office and making note of what exactly everyone is doing.
- The truth is, many “white-collar” administrative tasks can, and will, become automated soon enough, and this revolution will change what countless people around the world will be doing on weekdays between the hours of nine to five.
