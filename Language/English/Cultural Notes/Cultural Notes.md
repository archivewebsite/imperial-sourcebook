# Cultural Notes

These notes focus on communication habits around English, not one single country.

| Topic | Practical Note | Example | Indonesian Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small talk | Short social talk is often used before the main point. | `How was your weekend?` | Ini bukan selalu pertanyaan pribadi yang dalam. |
| Polite requests | Requests often use softeners. | `Could you send it today?` | Lebih halus daripada perintah langsung. |
| Directness | English can be direct but still softened by wording. | `I think we should revise this.` | `I think` mengurangi kesan keras. |
| Saying no | A direct `no` may be softened with reason or alternative. | `I can't today, but tomorrow works.` | Menolak sambil memberi opsi terdengar lebih sopan. |
| First names | Many English-speaking workplaces use first names quickly. | `Hi Sarah` | Tetap ikuti konteks sekolah, kantor, atau email resmi. |
| Please and thank you | These words matter in service and requests. | `Could I have a receipt, please?` | Tanpa `please` bisa terdengar terlalu tajam. |
| Apologies | `Sorry` can mean apology, sympathy, or interruption marker. | `Sorry, could I ask something?` | Tidak selalu berarti kesalahan besar. |
| Email tone | Opening and closing depend on relationship. | `Hi Alex`, `Best regards` | Jangan gunakan gaya chat untuk email resmi. |
| Personal space | Questions about salary, age, religion, or politics can feel too personal. | `Do you mind if I ask...?` | Pakai izin sebelum topik sensitif. |
| Idioms | Idioms can sound natural, but too many can sound forced. | `Let's call it a day.` | Pelajari untuk mengenali dulu. |
| Variety | British, American, Australian, and other varieties differ. | `flat` vs `apartment` | Pilih satu gaya untuk tulisan formal. |
| Classroom talk | It is normal to ask for clarification. | `Could you explain that again?` | Bertanya ulang bukan berarti gagal. |
