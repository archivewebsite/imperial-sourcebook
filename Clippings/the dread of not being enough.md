> will i ever be enough?

This question has long settled in the corner of my mind ever since I was a kid.

I was smart, never the smartest, but smart. My parents saw my potential, and they ran with it. They invested in me — ferried me to all the extra classes, bought me all sorts of books, signed me up to all these competitions I didn’t even know existed. But no matter how hard we all tried, I could never reach the top. I always ended up floating in the lower edge of the podium — close, but never there.

Academics were one thing. I also felt like I was not as pretty as the girls in my class. I felt like an ugly duckling in a pool of swans. They gathered attention without even trying, while ==I mastered the art of blending in so well that I became invisible.==

Not that good at sports either. I always ran out of breath too quickly. Not even games or any instrument. Unremarkable in all possible aspects.

Everywhere I looked, I saw that annoying two words hovering above my head: ***not enough.***

*not enough for others.  
never enough for yourself.*

Sometimes I try to argue with it, but most of the time I let it win, because maybe it’s right. Maybe I’ll never be enough.

The problem is that “enough” is a moving target — always travelling far from us. For every item that’s ticked off a bucket list, five shinier, louder goals pop up demanding our undivided attention: *“catch me, and then you’ll be free”*

It’s like chasing a train that accelerates whenever we get close. Always almost, but never quite. Yet, we chase it anyway.

I used to think this was greed — a hunger for more, more, more. But now I wonder if it’s something that’s carved into the architecture of being human. Maybe it’s an instinct passed down from our ancestors — the ones who hoarded everything to keep themselves alive.

==Maybe they also had a voice whispered in their ears that they were not running fast enough to escape the predators; that they didn’t have enough rice to feed the whole family; that they weren’t strong enough to survive in battles.==

It probably helped them survive. Maybe *“not enough”* was once a protective spell.

But somewhere along the way, it stops feeling like a lifeline and more like a weapon. The world no longer asks us to outrun dangers, yet the voice persists, telling us to outrun each other instead — be better, be faster, be more.

And we wonder why we feel tired.

==But who gets to define what== ==*“enough”*== ==should look like?==

For most of my life, I treated it as if it were an external law, carved into stone somewhere, unchangeable and universally agreed upon. I thought it lived out there — in other people’s approval, shiny achievements, and metrics I could display.

But now I’m starting to believe that it’s meant to be defined by us. Something that we create for ourselves — our own personal *enoughness*.

a good cup of coffee that awakens every cell inside us;  
a good book;  
a project you finish without comparing it to someone else’s masterpiece;  
a day when you finally smile at the person in the mirror instead of judging.

I’ll never silence the question entirely. The doubt will always sit in the corner of my mind, tapping its foot, wondering if I could be more. But now, instead of running toward the train that speeds away, I’m learning to sit on the platform.

> This is enough, I’d say.
> 
> ==enough love to keep going, enough grief to stay human, enough chaos to learn from, enough wonder to keep life vibrant, enough beauty to witness, enough strength to return to myself, again and again.==
> 
> I am enough.
