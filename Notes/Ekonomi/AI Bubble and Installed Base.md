## There is a strange feeling in the air right now if you have been around technology long enough.

> Sorry guys I am not responding to your comments, because there are tons of comments but don’t worry I am reading ya’ll comments 😊😊

==It feels familiar.== Too familiar. The kind of feeling you get right before a story stops making sense but everyone keeps repeating it anyway.

## Artificial intelligence has reached that phase.

The money came first. Then the headlines. Then the certainty. Every company suddenly had an AI strategy. Every startup pitch deck used the same words.

### Every investor pretended they understood transformer architectures after watching two YouTube videos and skimming one McKinsey report.

==That is usually how bubbles form. Not with lies, but with half truths stretched until they snap.==

## The promise versus the numbers

The core promise of this AI boom was productivity. That was the justification for the trillions. Machines that would write, code, design, diagnose, analyze, and replace entire categories of human labor.

## Now look at the actual data.

Multiple large scale studies from MIT, Stanford, and the National Bureau of Economic Research show that generative AI tools produce modest productivity gains in narrow tasks and often reduce output quality for experienced professionals.

Junior workers benefit slightly. ==Senior workers slow down correcting errors. Error rates in legal, medical, and financial outputs remain unacceptably high.==

## That is not disruption. That is assistance software with a marketing budget.

If AI were truly delivering transformative productivity, we would see it in macroeconomic numbers by now. Labor productivity growth in the United States and Europe remains flat. Corporate profit margins outside a handful of AI adjacent firms are not exploding. Costs are rising faster than value creation.

### This is the part people avoid discussing. AI is expensive. Inference costs are high.

> Training costs are astronomical. Energy consumption is brutal. Data center expansion is hitting physical and regulatory limits.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and similar labs burn billions per year. Their revenues do not even begin to cover the infrastructure required to run their models at scale. The business model relies on future breakthroughs that may not arrive.

That is not innovation. That is hope financed by venture capital.

## The ceiling nobody wants to admit exists

There is another uncomfortable truth. The improvements are slowing.

> **Early gains in AI felt magical because scaling laws were delivering visible jumps. More data and more compute produced noticeably better models. That curve is flattening.**

==Each new generation costs multiples more to train and delivers marginal improvements that only researchers can measure.== Hallucinations persist. Reasoning remains brittle. Context windows grow but understanding does not deepen in proportion.

## This is not a temporary obstacle. It is a structural one.

Large language models do not understand the world. They statistically approximate it. That approach has limits. You can smooth a curve forever, but you cannot turn correlation into comprehension through brute force alone.

The industry knows this. That is why the narrative is shifting.

## When belief weakens, a new story appears

Watch where the attention is going now.

> ==Quantum computing.==

Suddenly, quantum is no longer a long term research curiosity. It is being framed as the next inevitability. The missing piece. The escape hatch. The technology that will rescue AI from its constraints.

## This is where the cycle becomes obvious.

Quantum computing has existed as a theoretical and experimental field for decades. Its limitations are well known. Qubits are fragile. Error correction is massively complex. Scaling remains unsolved.

> Most quantum machines today cannot outperform classical computers on meaningful real world problems.

There are no usable quantum algorithms for training large AI models. There are no practical quantum advantages for chemistry, logistics, or cryptography at scale yet.

### Even optimistic roadmaps place general purpose quantum utility decades away.

None of that stops the hype machine.

### Because hype does not require readiness. It requires plausibility.

Quantum computing is abstract enough that most investors cannot evaluate it. That makes it perfect. You can promise breakthroughs without timelines. You can raise capital without products. You can shift attention without delivering results.

> This is not accidental. It is strategic.

When one bubble starts to wobble, capital looks for the next narrative that justifies continued spending. Quantum offers that narrative.

## The pattern never changes

This has happened before.

Railroads. Electricity. The internet. Blockchain. Metaverse. Now AI. Soon quantum.

> Each wave begins with genuine innovation. Then speculation overwhelms utility. Capital floods in faster than value can be created. Expectations detach from reality. Eventually, the story collapses under its own weight.

### The technology does not disappear. The excess does.

AI will not vanish. ==It will become boring==. It will settle into the background as software tools always do. Profitable in some areas. Useful in others. Nowhere near the omnipotent force it was sold as.

Quantum computing will follow the same path unless reality intervenes sooner. A few breakthroughs will happen quietly. Most companies will fail loudly. Investors will move on to the next abstract miracle.

## What this moment really reveals

This is not about AI or quantum computing. It is about incentives.

Markets reward narratives more than outcomes in the short term. Founders are pushed to promise futures they cannot deliver yet. Investors are afraid of missing the next big thing more than they are afraid of being wrong.

That is how bubbles are built. Not by stupidity, but by pressure.

==If you are watching this from the outside, the smartest move is not to cheer or panic. It is to observe. Strip away the story. Follow the cash flows. Measure results, not rhetoric.==

### Technological progress is slow, uneven, and unglamorous. Anyone promising inevitability is selling something.

The AI bubble is cracking because belief is meeting math. The next bubble is already forming because belief needs somewhere else to go.

History does not repeat itself exactly. It just keeps rhyming louder until people notice.

Most never do.
